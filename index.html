What am I looking at?

You are looking down on the Moon's south pole. The side facing Earth is at the top. Each numbered dot is a place NASA is studying for an astronaut landing. Tap a dot (or a name in the list) to choose it.

About this tool

The Lunar Landing Site Navigator helps mission planners, educators and the public compare landing sites near the Moon's south pole by showing where the Sun and the Earth are relative to the horizon.

Honest limitations (work in progress)

  • Site positions are approximate. NASA published the candidate regions as named areas about 15 km across; our markers are placed near their centres and labelled “approximate”.
  • Crater outlines are schematic circles from catalogue positions and sizes, not imagery.
  • Flat horizon. Real mountains and crater rims are not modelled yet. You can raise or lower the “local horizon height” to explore their effect.
